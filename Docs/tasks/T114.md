## Findings

- **Why the box bots looked like figures on a shelf:** the theater box model has no rail. Its balcony is a flat slab whose top (mesh z −40) runs from the drapes (mesh y ≈ 10) to a curved front edge (y 84.6 at the centre, back to 15.7 at x ±70); the slab's underside is at z −75. T106's bots stood on that top at y 35, so the whole bot, feet and all, showed above the box's front moulding. Measured from `Docs/Art/Set/theater_box/model.glb` with a pure-Python GLB reader (no numpy on this PC): the vertices' extents per slice, and the up- and down-facing triangles' areas per height, which told the slab's top from its underside.
- **What the boxes have now** (`Docs/tasks/T106-build/s08_box_seats.py`, in the outliner folder `Stage/Theater/Opera/T106/BoxSeats`, 408 actors in all: 97 parapet pieces, 2 chairs and a lamp of 3 pieces per box): on each slab's edge, 4 cm inside it, a parapet in 12 straight segments: a dark wood panel 30 tall, a gold moulding at its foot and top, fat gold turned balusters on its face (a shaft of radius 2.6 with a ball of radius 4.4, every 12 cm), and a crimson velvet rail (radius 5.5) with a velvet ball at each joint. The art direction asks for "classical but chunky, with nothing thin or spindly": a first try with thin balusters (radius 1.5, every 9 cm) read as a cage in the close-up. Behind the rail, each bot sits in a stalls seat (scale 0.55 × 0.5 wide and deep, height scaled so its back's top is 47 above the bot's pivot), lowered so 27 cm of it shows above the rail's top in the lower tier and 34 in the upper (the house camera is level with the upper rail, so less of a bot shows there), turned toward (−200, 0), the front of the stage, with a few degrees of jitter each. Under each arch hangs a lamp: a 6 cm glowing bulb on a gold rod, with a warm point light (5 cd, radius 230, no shadows) at the bulb. Without it the seated bots sank into a dark niche; with it, each box reads as a lit room with people in it. Two bots per box: a third would not fit the slab (about 140 wide) without crowding.
- **Frame time:** in PIE, four FPS charts of 12 s each, with the box seats (then 372 actors, with thinner balusters than now) hidden, shown, hidden, shown: 77.6, 54.7, 56.4 and 43.9 ms of GPU time. Each chart was lower than the one before it, whatever was shown, so the box seats cost less than this noise can show. The numbers are high and noisy because the editor ran in the background at 3 fps and drew its own 3376-wide viewport too; they say no more than that. Identical engine shapes with one material draw as instances, so the 300-odd gold pieces cost little.
- **Shots from a background editor (unreal MCP rules found in this task):** `AutomationLibrary.take_high_res_screenshot` and `HighResShot` from Python write nothing while the editor window is in the background, and `CaptureViewport` with a `captureTransform` renders at the viewport's own size with a FOV of its own (50° here), not the camera's. What works: PIE in a floating window, sized by `ObjectTools.set_properties` on `/Script/UnrealEd.Default__LevelEditorPlaySettings` (`values`: `{"newWindowWidth": 1600, "newWindowHeight": 900}`; Python can't set those properties), and `SlateInspector.Screenshot` of that window's ref. The result is about 2 MB of base64, so it lands in a tool-result file; `Saved/T114/ana/grab.sh` decodes the newest one and cuts the 1600 × 900 client area out with `System.Drawing` (no PIL on this PC). In PIE, a Python script moves the PIE world's `StageCamera` for close-ups and hides the UI with `unreal.ObjectIterator(unreal.UserWidget)` (`WidgetBlueprintLibrary` isn't exposed), and `PlayerController.set_view_target_with_blend` puts the title screen on the house camera.
- **Don't touch `Local/switches.json` while a show runs:** the game reads it again at each scene's Scene Prep (`NHOS: SWITCHES`). Setting `autoplay` back to false during a game window's autoplay show left scene 2 at `NHOS: PREP shown` with no `PREP start`, waiting for a player; the show had to be run again. Set it back only after `NHOS: SHOW END`.
- **Worktree sessions can't write the repo folder:** this session started in a worktree, and the desktop app refuses `Write` and `Edit` on files in the repo folder from it. The scripts and docs were written in the worktree and run from there (`py "<worktree>/Docs/tasks/T106-build/s08_box_seats.py"`; `t106lib` then writes to the worktree's `Saved/T106/out/`), and the editor saved `L_Stage` in the repo folder.
- **The check, as a first-time player would see it:** before, the box bots stood full height on bare shelves at the frame's edges, like figurines. Now each box reads as an opera box: a lit room behind a gold balustrade, two patrons in top hats (one bare-headed) peering over a velvet rail at the stage, crimson chair backs behind them. From the house camera at 1600 × 900 the lower boxes show the rail, the lamp's glow and the patrons' heads and shoulders; the upper boxes, cut by the frame's top, show the rail and the patrons' heads. Nothing in a box reaches the stage. At 1440 × 900 the camera crops the sides and only the lower boxes' inner edges show (a rail and a head). A PIE show (Test for Free, autoplay, a 1600 × 900 floating window, UI on) was screenshot in the scene turn, Free Talk, the Last Word and The Cut, and one at 1440 × 900 in a scene turn: the UI panels sit over the upper boxes as before, and the boxes never clash with a bubble or a name tag. No `LogScript` line in the PIE shows. Shots are in `Saved/T114/shots/` (not in git): `before/`, `v1`..`v4/` (each try), and `v4/` holds the final close-up, the house frames and the phase frames.
- **Not judged from screenshots:** the boxes are static; whether the seated bots should also move (a sway, a head turn, a clap at the cut) is my person's call (step 6), since it changes `BP_AudienceBot`.
