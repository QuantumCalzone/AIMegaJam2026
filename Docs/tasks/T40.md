# T40 Generate the 13 headpieces

Back to [the plan](../Plan.md). This task's title line, **Model and effort**, **Depends on**, **Needs**, **Touches** and any **Waiting:** or **Blocked:** line are in its entry there, under "Open".

## Task

- **Why:** every role in the seven stories wears one of 13 clay headpieces (the design doc's "Art Inspiration" tab, Costumes). Each piece is one model, colored per role by a clay material, so the models are made without a texture.
- **Steps:**
  1. `python Tools/art_gen.py batch-images --kind headpiece`, then look at each asset's four candidates and `pick` the clearest one: the whole piece in frame, seen from three-quarters, nothing else in the picture, and a shape that sits on a round head. Redo the images for any asset with no good candidate (a second `batch-images --ids ...`).
  2. `python Tools/art_gen.py batch-meshes --kind headpiece --max-credits 300`. Mesh only (`texture` false): 20 credits each, 260 in all.
  3. `sheet --kind headpiece`, and check every thumbnail: a clean, closed shape, no stray parts, the brim or opening where a head goes. Redo at most three that fail.
  4. Add a row for each to `Docs/Assets.md`, under "Generated 3D models", as the plush horse head's row does.
  5. Show me the sheet.
- **Done when:** 13 FBX files are in `Docs/PropArt/<id>/`, each with its log and an `Assets.md` row, and I say the set looks right.

## Findings

- **Run (2026-11-15).** First `batch-images` gave usable candidates for 8 of 13. Picks: top_hat 2, ball_cap 1, wide_brim_hat 3, saucepan_helmet 3, fedora 4, horned_helmet_beard 4, dino_hood 1, beanie 3. `batch-meshes` made all 13 on `meshy-7.1` without a texture at 20 credits each: 260 in all, balance 1,025 to 765, as the logs say. No model failed the sheet check, so none was redone.
- **"Sized as a hat for a small capsule-shaped robot" draws the robot.** For the hood, the sunglasses, the bob wig, the wizard hat and the tiara, every first candidate had a ball or head under the piece, which Meshy would have made part of the mesh. Their prompts in the manifest now say what the piece rests on and name what must not be there ("an empty hat resting flat on its brim on the floor. No head, no face, no ball and nothing under it"), and drop the robot line. The second batch was clean for four of them (hood 4, bob_wig 1, wizard_hat 1, tiara 4). The sunglasses still came on a cartoon body with arms and legs; a third prompt, "a product photo of a single pair of ... sunglasses ... lying on a table. Only the glasses: no head, no face, no character, no body, no hands, no legs", worked (dark_shades 3). T41 and T42 props shown "on" something may need the same wording.
- **What the models look like (thumbnails).** Hood, bob wig and dino hood keep a real opening at the front (the dino's face opening is its mouth). The horned helmet keeps the small round face between the nose guard and the beard, as its reference has it, so on a robot that face covers the robot's own. The dino hood kept only one small crest of its row of spikes. The beanie's knit is lumpy at 15,000 triangles; at headpiece size on stage it should read as knit. The tiara came out crown-shaped, with points and round gems.
- **For T43 and T47.** Meshy scales every model to about 1.9 m (T39's style test), so each needs a scale of about 0.13 for 25 cm. The thumbnails show only the top; whether each underside is open or filled shows once T43 imports them. T47's plan leaves the robot's antenna topper poking up through the headpiece, and Meshy usually makes closed solids, so the topper may end up hidden inside a hat unless T47 moves it or cuts a hole.
- **Contact sheets without packages.** This Mac's Python has no PIL and there is no ImageMagick. A 20-line Swift script with AppKit (`swiftc`) tiles PNGs into one image, which is faster to judge than 52 separate reads. The labeled headpiece sheet is `Local/art/headpiece_sheet.png` (not in git).
