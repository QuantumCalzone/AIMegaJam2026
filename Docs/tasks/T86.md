# T86 A full house: more rows of audience at the bottom of the screen

Back to [the plan](../Plan.md). This task's title line, **Model and effort**, **Depends on**, **Needs**, **Touches** and any **Waiting:** or **Blocked:** line are in its entry there, under "Open".

## Task

- **Why:** my note on 2026-11-15: the bottom part of the screen has visible empty spaces, and we don't want the house to look barren. T49 put one row of stalls across the bottom of the house camera's frame: 13 crimson seat backs at x −470 with a gold rail, and only 7 audience bots in them. Below that row, the frame shows bare dark floor down to its bottom edge, and the bottom-right corner beside the lower boxes is bare too. T65's `Saved/T65/shots/freetalk_at.png` (on the Mac) shows it: the chat panel covers the bottom left, and the bottom middle and right show one half-empty row and then empty floor.
- **Steps:**
  1. **Look first.** Take screenshots from the house camera at 1600 × 900 in Scene Prep, a scene turn, Free Talk and the vote, and one from the title screen's camera (T49's `t0.png` showed it frames the house too). Mark where bare floor shows in the bottom third of the frame, and which of it the UI panels cover. If T84 is done, scene 1's Scene Prep hides the lower panel, so more of the floor shows there.
  2. **More rows.** Add rows of T49's crimson seat backs (`MI_Theater_Velvet`, the same size) between T49's row and the house camera, at a real row spacing for this scale, until no bare floor shows at the bottom of the frame. A row nearer the camera spans more of the floor, so make each row wide enough to reach both edges of the frame at its depth. Keep the tops of every row below the stage's front edge on screen: nothing may cover a robot, a name label, a place, the prop table, the Riser, the backdrop or a speech bubble. A center aisle or a gold rail per row is your call, if it reads better.
  3. **Fill the seats.** Put a `BP_AudienceBot` in most seats (about four in five, T49's row too; a few empty seats look real, rows of them look barren), seen from behind like T49's. Vary them so the crowd doesn't look stamped out: spread `MI_Clay_AudRose`, `AudPlum`, `AudMustard`, `AudBrick` and `AudLilac` so no two neighbors match, turn and scale each bot a little at random (about ±15° and 0.37 to 0.43), lean a few toward a neighbor, and give some opera glasses. If a top hat on every head looks uniform, put some of T40's other headpieces on, or none. Add an `MI_Clay_Aud*` color on `M_Clay` if five are too few, never a seat color.
  4. **Light.** Rows nearer the camera get less of the stage's spill and may go black. The crowd should read as dim silhouettes, with colored domes and hat brims that catch a little light. If they don't, add a faint house light that casts no shadow and doesn't reach the stage. The robots stay the brightest things on screen, and `StagePost`'s exposure and the stage lights don't change.
  5. **Keep it cheap.** No collision on anything new. Measure the frame time in PIE (`stat unit`) before and after. If it rises noticeably, turn off shadows on the audience parts first, then use fewer bots in the rows the UI panels cover.
  6. **Place it by script, apart from T49's pieces.** T49's `Saved/T49/layout.py` (on the Mac only, not in git) destroys and places again everything under `Stage/Theater/Opera/`, so don't run it as it is. Write a re-runnable script that destroys and places only its own folder, for example `Stage/Theater/Opera/StallsMore`, so each try is one call and T49's pieces stay as they are.
  7. **Check.** The same screenshots as step 1, at 1600 × 900 and at 1440 × 900. The camera keeps a 90° horizontal field of view, so a taller window shows more floor at the bottom. Also run a free autoplay show in a game window on the saved assets until it reaches `NHOS: SHOW END`. If the design doc's Art Inspiration tab ("The stage") describes the audience and no longer fits, show me the edit (see "The design doc follows the game" in the plan).
- **Done when:** no bare floor shows at the bottom of the frame in any of the step 7 screenshots at either size; the house reads as full from behind; nothing on stage is covered; the frame time is about what it was; a free autoplay show reaches `NHOS: SHOW END`; and Claude judges from the screenshots, as a critical first-time player would, that the bottom of the screen no longer looks barren, and writes what it saw under "## Findings".
