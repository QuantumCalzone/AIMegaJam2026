## Findings

- **Fits in the data are in `HeadSocket` space, and that space differs per robot.** T103 put Meta's socket at z 151 with scale 0.9 and mine at (-2, 0, 162) with scale 1.1. The build sheet's fits were relative to each head's top at scale 1, so the data divides Meta's and my fits by the socket's scale and moves them by its offset (`Saved/T104/drafts/make_costumes.py` on the Mac does it). Without that, every costume on Meta and on me sits at the wrong size.
- **The glTF imports face +Y,** as T103 found. Every fit's `yaw` is 270 plus its own turn, and `scale3` is in the mesh's own axes (across, front, up). A roll of 180 (my saucepan, upside down) needs a yaw of the turn plus 90, not 270.
- **Rodin scales every model to 1.9 m on its longest side.** Bounds come in at ±95 cm on one axis whatever the costume, so the scales in the fits (0.3 to 0.8) do all the sizing.
- **Fixes by eye, from the build sheet's fits:** my dino hood up 28.5 (z -43 to -14.5), so my face shows in its mouth; SpaceXAI's bob wig up 16.6 (z -28.6 to -12), so the bangs clear its visor; the beard down on SpaceXAI (z -85.3 to -96) and Meta (z -93.2 to -106), so it hangs below their mouths; the dino hood on SpaceXAI taller (`scale3` up 0.4893 to 0.72) and back 10 cm, and on Meta taller and back 18 cm (x 2.1 to -16), so the lower jaw stops hiding their mouths. Each `top` moved with its `z`.
- **The dino hood's lower jaw hides a low mouth by parallax.** Its lip sits in front of the face, and the house camera looks down 20°, so the jaw covers the mouth even when the opening is tall enough. Moving the hood back, so the lips sit level with the face, fixed it; raising it made it worse.
- **Three old headpieces stay:** `Content/NHOS/Stage/Headpieces/SM_Beanie` and `SM_Fedora` (the audience in `L_Stage`) and `SM_TopHat` (`Stage/Set/BP_AudienceBot`). Those are T106's files, so T104 left them; nothing in `BP_Robot` or the data uses them. T106 can move the audience onto the new costume meshes (or its own) and delete the folder. The cook list drops `Headpieces` for `Costumes`; the three still cook because the map uses them.
- **Autoplay skips Scene Prep.** `OpenPrep` reads the switches and calls `StartFromPrep` at once when autoplay is on, so an autoplay run takes off the costumes and puts on the next ones in the same frame. For Scene Prep shots, the PIE show ran with autoplay off; a Slate tick script took the shot, set autoplay on, called `BP_Show.LoadSwitches` and `StartFromPrep`, and set it off again after each cut (`Saved/T104/py/pieshow.py`).
- **Editor Python, new this task:** `BlueprintTools.add_variable(..., container_type=ContainerType.ARRAY)` (import `ContainerType` from `editor_toolset.toolsets.blueprint`) makes an array; without it you get a plain variable. `Json|GetField` into a Float array variable reads a JSON number array (`FitScale`). `Json|GetFieldNames` exists and lists an object's keys (`CostumeTestInit`). "Use Less CPU when in Background" is `bThrottleCPUWhenNotForeground` on `/Script/UnrealEd.Default__EditorPerformanceSettings`; turning it off keeps Slate tick scripts on time while the editor is in the background. `HighResShot 1600x900` run in the PIE world saves the game view to `Saved/Screenshots/MacEditor/`.
- **The game-window capture stopped after 90 s:** `winid` found no window from then on, though the show played on to its end (the log has every scene). The PIE show gave the frames instead.
