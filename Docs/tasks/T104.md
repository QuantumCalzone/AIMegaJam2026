# T104 New art, part 8: the costumes' models, fitted to every character

Back to [the plan](../Plan.md). This task's title line, **Model and effort**, **Depends on**, **Needs**, **Touches** and any **Waiting:** or **Blocked:** line are in its entry there, under "Open".

## Task

- **Why:** my request on 2026-11-15: the costumes must actually fit the characters, mine included, and my robot's head is a cube. T96 got my approval on a costume for each role and on how it fits each of the five characters, with a clean view of each version a model needs. T103 put the new cast on the robots. This task makes the costume models and fits each one to each robot.
- **What changes in the data.** Today `costumes.json` gives each headpiece one mesh and one fit that every robot uses (T47). From now on, each costume has a mesh per version (round heads, Meta's head and the cube, or as T96 decided), and a fit per character: the scale, the offset and the yaw. `scenes.json` names each role's costume and color, from T96's role table.
- **What must keep working:**
  - Each robot takes off its costume at Scene Prep and puts on its seat's costume when the scene starts, by watching `BP_Show`'s `SceneNum` and `Phase` itself (`BP_Robot.WatchShow`, `DressFromShow`, `SetHeadpiece`).
  - A cut robot keeps its costume.
  - Nothing in this task changes `BP_Show`.
- **Steps:**
  1. **Read first:** T96's README and role table in `Docs/Art/Costumes/`; T103's findings on each head; `Docs/tasks/T47.md` (how dressing works); `Content/NHOS/Data/costumes.json` and the seats in `scenes.json`; and the `BP_Robot` graphs `DressFromShow` and `SetHeadpiece` (read only the graphs you change).
  2. **Generate.** Make one model per version from T96's clean views, with Meshy (`Tools/art_gen.py mesh-image`, the PC's `Local/meshy_key.json`) or Hyper3D (the Mac's MCP server, or its API with a key that I put in `Local/`). Tell me which one and why. Before the first paid call, tell me the estimated credits and ask once. Ask again only if the cost would go over that. Save each model, its thumbnail and its log in `Docs/Art/Costumes/<costume>/<version>/`. Check each model against its view. Show me a thumbnail sheet, ask for my OK, and regenerate what I reject.
  3. **Import.** Import into `Content/NHOS/Stage/Costumes/<costume>/`, on a Substrate material. A costume part that isn't on the head, such as a collar or a cape, gets the socket or attach point it needs on `BP_Robot`.
  4. **The fits.** Write `costumes.json` in its new form: each costume's versions, and each character's version and fit. Update its `_readme`. Change `SetHeadpiece` (and `DressFromShow`, if it must) to read the fit for this robot's character. Write `scenes.json`'s seats from T96's table. Update `Tools/check_prompts.py`'s costume check, if it has one, to the new form.
  5. **A test switch.** `"costume_test": true` in `Local/switches.json` dresses every robot in each costume in turn, a few seconds each, as `emote_test` does for emotions. Add it to `Docs/switches.example.json`.
  6. **Fit by eye.** With `costume_test`, take a screenshot of each costume on all five robots at the house camera, plus a close shot of the heads. Fix each fit until nothing floats, clips into the head or face, or covers the eyes, the face or the name tag. Make a grid of the final shots (costumes by characters), and show it to me for my OK next to T96's fit sheets.
  7. **Clean up and check.** Delete the old headpieces in `Content/NHOS/Stage/Headpieces/` and their materials once nothing uses them. Play a PIE show (Test for Free, 1600 × 900): take a screenshot of each scene's Scene Prep (no costumes) and its first turn (each robot in its seat's costume), and of a cut robot that keeps its costume. Play a free autoplay show in a game window to `NHOS: SHOW END`.
  8. **Record it.** In `Docs/Assets.md`, replace the headpiece rows with the new costume models: the tool, the generation ids and the license. Show me the edit to the design doc's Costumes section (a capture of the final grid), and make it after my OK, in its file in `Docs/Design/`.
- **Done when:**
  1. Every role's costume sits right on every character, my cube head included, in the grid I approved.
  2. `costumes.json` has a fit per character, `scenes.json` names each role's costume, and the robots dress and undress in a show as before.
  3. The old headpieces are deleted, and nothing references them.
  4. A free autoplay show reaches `NHOS: SHOW END`.
  5. `Docs/Assets.md`, `Docs/Art/Costumes/` and the design doc record the new costumes.
