## Findings

- **What the PIE shots show.** The desert backdrop (`Saved/T99/shots/02_prep.png` and `03_scene.png` on the Mac that ran it) reads at once behind the robots: hut left, truck right, the sly moon above, and a plain sand floor where the robots stand, so nothing fights them. The dark backdrops hold up on stage: the cubicles' green screens and rainy window, and the dwarf mine's candles and glowing arch, read behind the robots without washing them out. A freshly swapped backdrop is blurry for about a second while its mips stream in. The bill at 120 × 60 reads as money, the badge sits exactly on the medallion, the "100"s are small but legible, and a stack of bills shows the char wipe with clay flames at its edge. The earpiece's 66 px portrait reads as a little trash can with a lens; the earpiece made from the script sat partly under the title card, but its place is T61's to set, not this task's.
- **Seat-colored badges.** My person chose seat colors over the old single sage color, so a burning bill now tells whose call it paid at a glance. Meta's badge follows T108's #C8AC12; if a seat color changes again, redraw that badge with `mb.swift` and reimport it (seconds, no credits).
- **The generated bill's medallion was off center.** The widget draws the badge at the bill's exact center, and generated art doesn't know that, so `mb.swift` mirrors the bill's left half about the medallion. Check any generated art against the widget's fixed anchors before import.
- **The stagehand took six rounds.** Glowing eyes read as menacing ("Those are all very creepy."), cartoon eyes turned it into a porcelain doll, and negative prompts didn't keep noses and mouths off. A subject with no head shape (a trash can) solved it. My person's references (WALL-E, Star Wars droids, Ralph McQuarrie) went into the prompts only as described qualities, with an original design asked for every time.
- **Editor Python and widgets.** `unreal.WidgetBlueprintLibrary` isn't exposed in this editor's Python, and `PlayerController.add_component_by_class` isn't either. `unreal.new_object(BPC_Stagehand_C, outer=pc)` and `call_method("MakeEar")` worked: the component's own `CreateWidget` made the earpiece in PIE, and `call_method("Play", (line, seconds))` showed it.
- **Clicks in the PIE window.** After a command in the editor's Cmd box, `Click` on a game button didn't land, even after `Windows select`. A `Type` of one space into the game's input box first, then the `Click`, worked (as T64 found).
- **Left for later:** the itch.io page's cover and screenshots still show the old art (not in this task). `Docs/BackdropArt.md`, `Docs/MoneyBurnArt.md` and `Docs/StagehandArt/` keep the first sets' story; nothing in the game uses those files now.
