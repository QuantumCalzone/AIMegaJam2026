## Findings

### T56: rivals

- **Where the two `game` lines sit.** The doc's two lines are copied word for word as sub-bullets: "Your persona names a rival. ..." under goal 1 (it only breaks a tie between two suspects, so finding the human stays first) and "You also want to reach the final two yourself." under goal 2, which it agrees with. The doc's block has them as plain bullets among the other new rules; the rest of that block (the note category, silence as evidence, the tie rule) belongs to T50, T52, T57 and the others, so T56 copied only its own two lines. A later task that copies the whole block may move these two in with it.
- **The rival lines.** Copied word for word as each prompt's last line. They describe the rival by its traits, with no persona id, provider name, pun name or Player name. The `rival` field sits after `provider` and, like it, never reaches the AIs (the system text uses only `prompt`).
- **`check_prompts.py`.** New checks: every persona has a `rival` that is another persona's id; the pairs are mutual; the prompt ends with a line starting "Your rival: "; that line holds no persona id, provider name, old persona name, doc pun name (new `PUN_NAMES` list) or Player name. The banned-name scan of the prompt files now also catches the pun names. Each check was tried on a broken copy of the data (self rival, unknown rival, missing rival, not mutual, no rival line, an id, a provider name, a pun name and a Player name in the line): each failed as it should.
- **Not checked (needs a paid run, which I didn't make).** Step 3, whether a bot whose rival was cut talks as if the rival were still there, and step 5, reading 10 votes for a vote against the evidence, need real replies; `--relay --free` uses canned lines. Things to watch for in that run:
  - In a 4-actor show one persona sits out, so its rival has no one to match. A bot may then pin its rival's traits on whoever looks closest, possibly the human faking a persona; that is fine for the game, but a bot naming a missing rival as present would read oddly.
  - "You'd love to see it cut" is stronger than the `game` line's "lean toward your rival" when undecided. If paid runs show bots voting for their rival against the evidence, soften the rival lines in the doc first.
  - The overclocker's persona already says it "announces a new rival" when ignored. The two uses of "rival" may blur; watch whether it treats its fixed rival and its announced rivals apart.
  - The rival line tells each AI that a bot with those traits is an AI, which is a small clue against a human who fakes that persona. It matches the design, but it makes faking the anxious or the stiff bot slightly riskier.
