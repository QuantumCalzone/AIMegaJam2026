## Findings

### Tool and cost

- **Meshy had 60 credits left** (2 props' worth; the task's estimate was 840), so my person chose Hyper3D Rodin with a cap of 30 credits. T81 had already checked that its Business plan lets us ship the output.
- **35 paid Rodin calls, Gen-2.5 Medium:** 28 first takes, the horse head again (take 1 came back as 10,016 scattered slivers with a total area of 0.006), the cannoli three times, the envelope twice, the phone twice. At T81's measured 0.5 credit a call, about 17.5 credits. The MCP tools report no cost per call; `t103-prep-cast-models` spent from the same wallet at the same time, so the balance can't separate them.
- **Settings:** one reference image (the approved `model_view.png`), a prompt (T97's subject plus a clay line and T97's notes: matte silver-gray tin can, plain seal, dark old-gold rug diamonds, plain green bills, no phone cord, opaque milkshake, no light rays), Raw mesh, 15,000 triangles (8,000 for the ring, pills, spoon, waiver, envelope and watch), FBX. `texture_delight` on for the shiny-looking ones (tin can, ring, pan, cannoli tray, watch, spoon).
- **Getting the files:** `rodin_get_result` gives signed URLs; T105 downloaded `base_basic_pbr.fbx` (it embeds diffuse, normal, roughness and metallic maps) and `texture_diffuse.png`. Each result listing is about 5,000 tokens of URLs; `Docs/Art/Props/t105_tools/` has the scripts that build the URL from its signature.

### What the image wins over the prompt

- Rodin copies the reference image's details even when the prompt says otherwise: the envelope's seal mark (painted and raised both), the phone's curly cord, the bowling ball's pale lavender. Moving the note to the front of the prompt didn't help.
- **A gray object on the light gray background vanishes:** the cannoli's gray tray was left out in all three takes, even text-only. T105 added a plain round tray to take 1 itself (below).
- Rodin's front faces −Y in the FBX's own coordinates, but **the FBX import mirrors Y**, so in Unreal it faces +Y, as Meshy's did: yaw 90 turns a prop to the house. (A first try at −90 showed the tin can's back.)
- Rodin added an emissive map on its own for the staff's crystal; it is the staff's glow mask.

### Fixes made here, with my person's OK on each

- `t105_tools/fbxedit.py`: a stdlib reader and writer for binary FBX 7.4 that keeps every unchanged array byte for byte (a plain re-encode wasn't accepted by the FBX SDK; the culprit was a one-byte slip at the final null record). It smoothed the seal's front vertices (171 vertices, 40 Laplacian passes) and added the cannoli tray (480 triangles, UVs in an empty corner of the diffuse map, painted pale gray).
- `t105_tools/fixtex.swift`: the bowling ball recolored to deep plum, the rug's diamonds to dark old gold, the seal disc painted plain, and the glow masks for the sword's blade and the briefcase's lining (the briefcase mask also catches its two small gold latch plates).
- Every final FBX carries the fixed diffuse map, and its normal, roughness and metallic maps are flat 1-pixel images: the clay stays matte, the normal map can't bring back the seal's relief, and the 28 files total about 75 MB.
- The tin can stayed near white (de-lit) rather than silver-gray; my person approved it as it is.

### Import (editor)

- **The MCP import tools won't replace an asset** (`already exists`), and deleting first would have broken `L_Stage`'s references to the four space_plans meshes (its prop actors are saved with them). An editor Python script run from the Cmd box (`py "<file>"`, T46's way) with `AssetImportTask(replace_existing=True, automated=True)` reimported each texture over `T_<Name>_BaseColor` and each FBX over `SM_<Name>`, kept `M_<Name>` (a plain texture sample into Base Color), and deleted the material and textures the FBX import made. The Interchange importer is on; the old props' `FbxStaticMeshImportData` didn't get in the way.
- **A new mesh comes in with Nanite on** (the bowl of oranges); `get_num_triangles` then reports the fallback mesh (2,434). Turned off to match the others.
- **Glow:** a `TextureSample(T_<Name>_Glow) x color x strength` into Emissive Color on `M_GlowStickSword` (mint, 6), `M_GlowingStaff` (icy white, 8) and `M_GlowingBriefcase` (warm gold, 3). The mask textures are linear (sRGB off), so the sampler must be **Linear Grayscale**: with Grayscale the material fails to compile on Metal and renders as the default gray, which the first PIE shots showed.
- `props.json`: `scale = size_cm / longest side`, `z = -min_z x scale`, `grip_z = -half height x scale`, yaw 90, except the toy blaster and the pan (0, side on) and the piggy bank (60, three-quarter front). **The sword grips at its handle (−9) and the staff at a third of its height (−30)**, not at the middle, so their tops stand above the hand; the sword and the staff stand upright on the table, as their approved views do.

Nothing was saved from PIE.

From the house camera the table is behind SpaceXAI and Meta at the opening marks (as T45 found), so the props read once a robot holds or moves one. A held prop is the right size next to the robots.

A prop is centered on the socket at its `grip_z`, so a tall prop held by a short-armed robot reaches the floor.
