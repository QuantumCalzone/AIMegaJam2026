### Design review, as a critical first-time player

- **The faces add to the show.** The face changes in the same frame as the bubble appears, so face, bubble and voice read as one moment, and the gestures carry from the house camera even where the face itself is small: a lean-in on an accusation, a tremble on a worried line, a bounce on a happy one, folded arms on a smug one. A vote read out with a suspicious lean-in sells the accusation.
- **The faces are small at 1600 × 900.** Each face screen is about 40 pixels across, so the eyes read best when I look for them; the gesture is what a player notices first. That suits a show where the chat carries the words.
- **The dark shades hide the face.** T47's `dark_shades` headpiece covers the face screen's eyes, so a robot wearing it shows only the gesture (OpenAI in run 2, my robot in run 1). It is one of 14 headpieces and the gesture still plays, so it is no tell, but a later task could let the shades show the eyes (or lift them) while a face plays.
- **Free mode's faces sometimes fight the line.** Its picks are random by phase weight, so "Behold, the safety waiver. It is the key to this whole story." played angry. The real bots pick from their own line, so this belongs to Test for Free only.
- **Faces change often in Free Talk,** one per line, each held 2.5 to 3.5 s (T75), and ease back between lines; with the typing and the bubble it doesn't feel busy.
- **The cut robot's last words** (decision 17) have a bubble and a still face. It reads as a robot already switched off, which fits the cut; not changed.
- **My robot is no tell.** In PIE my lines got embarrassed, suspicious, confused, smug and surprised, with the same gestures and timing as the bots. In the free autoplay show my robot was neutral on 9 of 28 lines (32%), the bots on 2 to 6 of 13 to 29 (8 to 22%). The gap comes from my two canned passes and canned lines with no emotion words ("This is mine now. I think. Probably."). With real typing it follows the player's words and the input LLM (neutral on 9 of 18 test lines, all of them lines with no emotion). If real play shows my robot neutral much more often than the bots, the fix is more `emotion_keywords` words or a sentence in `input` asking for a face whenever the line shows one; that would need my person's OK on the doc edit.

### Unreal MCP rules found in this task

- **`add_function_param` takes only plain types** (bool, int, float, byte, name, string, text, Vector, Rotator, Transform, Vector2D, LinearColor). A `JsonObjectWrapper` param is refused ("Unknown type"); pass a String instead, or read the JSON in the caller.
- **`add_variable` takes `container_type` `ARRAY`,** and `add_struct_variable` takes it too, so an array of `JsonObjectWrapper` is `add_struct_variable` with `struct_type` `/Script/JsonUtilities.JsonObjectWrapper` and `container_type` `ARRAY`. `add_object_variable` with `/Game/NHOS/Stage/BP_Robot.BP_Robot_C` makes a robot reference.
- **A `write_graph_dsl` of `BPC_LLMClient.FreeReply` took over 110 s** and outran the client's wait, but the editor finished it. Watch the editor log for its next `Compiling Blueprint` line before the next call, or give the HTTP client a longer timeout (590 s worked for the rest of `BP_Show`).
- **From Git Bash, a `/Game/...` argument becomes `C:/Program Files/Git/Game/...`** (MSYS path conversion), so `save_assets` reports "Asset does not exist". Run the script with `MSYS_NO_PATHCONV=1`.
- **The Slate snapshot's prompt text is not "YOUR TURN" in a scene turn** ("Turn 2 of 3. YOUR MOVE: ..."), and "Your turn comes next." shows while the lines before mine play. `NHOS: TIMER start` in the log is the reliable sign that my scene turn's input is open (T66). A PIE driver that reads the log should also skip lines older than about 1.5 s, or a shot catches a line that has already gone.
- **The free AI passes are logged (`NHOS: ENTRY ... "passes"`) when they reach the log, before they play,** so match a pass's `NHOS: EMOTE` only after its phase's `CUE phase` line has played.
