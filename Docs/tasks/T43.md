- **Depends on:** T40, T41, T42 (it can import one kind as soon as its task is done)

- **Touches:** new `Content/NHOS/Stage/Headpieces/`, `Content/NHOS/Stage/Set/`, new folders in `Content/NHOS/Stage/Props/`, new `Content/NHOS/Stage/Materials/M_Clay.uasset` and its instances, `Docs/PropArt/manifest.json`, `Docs/Submission.md`, `Docs/tasks/T43.md`.
- **Why:** T40 to T42 leave FBX files in `Docs/PropArt/`. This task makes them assets the stage can use, and makes the one clay material that colors the headpieces and anything built from engine shapes. It places nothing in the game: the tasks that hook the art in (see "Not planned yet" in the plan) use these assets.
- **Steps:** follow "Import and look" and "Test result" in [Docs/PropArt.md](../PropArt.md). Unreal MCP calls run one at a time; split the work into short calls.
  1. **Import each model** in the manifest that has an `fbx` and no asset yet, with `StaticMeshTools.import_file`:
     - props into `/Game/NHOS/Stage/Props/<card id>/`, headpieces into `/Game/NHOS/Stage/Headpieces/`, set pieces into `/Game/NHOS/Stage/Set/`, each as `SM_<Name>`.
     - Props and set pieces with materials and textures; rename `Material_001` and `texture_0` to `M_<Name>` and `T_<Name>_BaseColor` straight after each import, or the next one collides.
     - Headpieces without materials.
     - Move each `<name>.fbm/` folder the importer leaves beside an FBX into `Local/art/props/<id>/`.
  2. **Record the fit** in the manifest for each model: its bounds, the scale that gives its `size_cm` (Meshy scales everything to about 1.9 m), and the yaw that turns its face to the house (the horse head needed 90).
  3. **Make `M_Clay`** in `/Game/NHOS/Stage/Materials/`: Substrate, matte (roughness about 0.85), a `Color` parameter and a faint noise in the normal for thumbprints. Put an instance in a neutral color on every headpiece.
  4. **Look at them:** place every model in a row on the stage as temporary actors at its recorded scale and yaw, take a screenshot from the house camera and one close up, then delete the actors. Don't save `L_Stage`.
  5. Save the new assets. They are all new, so nothing needs a lock.
  6. In `Docs/Submission.md`, add Leonardo.ai and Meshy to "Tools you used", and rewrite the lines that say no AI image or 3D tools were used and that there is no AI-generated art.

## Findings

### What the editor showed (E1 to E10)

- **`import_file` returns only the mesh.** The material and texture it makes are not in `returnValue`. They are in the folder as `Material_001` and `texture_0` (the same names every time). The batch script found them with `AssetTools.find_assets` in the folder right after each import and moved them to `M_<Name>` and `T_<Name>_BaseColor` before the next import. `move` kept the mesh slot and the texture sample pointing at the renamed assets.
- **Imports are fast:** about 0.1 s per FBX in the log. Batches of 10 to 14 models ran in one `execute_tool_script` well under the time limit.
- **Material property names are camelCase** (`parameterName`, `defaultValue`, `constant`, `r`, `noiseFunction`, `levels`, `outputMin`, `outputMax`, `bTurbulence`). The Noise node's input is `World Position`, not `Position`. A VectorParameter's outputs are `RGB`, `R`, `G`, `B`, `A`, `RGBA`; `RGB` goes into the Slab's `Diffuse Albedo`. `M_Robot` feeds `MP_FrontMaterial` from a Slab, so `M_Clay` does the same. `get_expression_inputs` needs `material_or_function` as well as `expression`.
- **`find_actors` needs `name`, `tag` and `collision_channels`** (pass `''`, `''`, `[]`). **`CaptureViewport` needs `annotations`** even when unused: `{gridSpacing: 0, gridExtent: 0, gridHeight: 0, maxLabelDistance: 0, classFilter: {refPath: '/Script/Engine.StaticMeshActor'}, maxLabels: 0}`.
- **Actors placed in the same script as a capture often don't render yet:** the capture shows their shadows but not the meshes. Place them in one call and capture in the next.
- **This Mac's editor log** is `~/Library/Logs/Unreal Engine/AIMegaJam2026Editor/AIMegaJam2026.log`, not `Saved/Logs/`.
- **No `.fbm` folders** were left beside the FBX files.

### The fit

- **Every Meshy model faces +Y**, so its yaw is 90 to face the house (as the horse head's was). Checked with four turned copies each for the hood, sunglasses, ball cap, tiara, horned helmet, dino hood and bob wig, and on the props row (pills, rug, phone, briefcase, piggy bank and the set pieces all face the house at 90). The manifest records yaw 90 for all 43.
- **Meshy scales every model to about 190 cm on its longest side** (the horse head was 190, not the 176 the sheet worked back). Headpieces are scaled by footprint to 25 cm (open question 1, as recommended): 0.131 for most, 0.140 for the hood, 0.152 for the beanie, 0.177 for the horned helmet. Props are 30 cm on the longest side (0.158); set pieces 0.948 (chandelier, 180 cm), 0.315 (cherub, 60 cm), 1.577 (theater box, 300 cm). An actor sits on a floor at `Z0` at `z = Z0 - min_z * scale`.
- **Every prop is 30 cm** because that is each one's `size_cm` in the manifest. It reads well for most, but some look big for what they are: the cursed ring is as big as the bowling ball, the pills, spoon and pocket watch are 30 cm long, and the glow-stick sword and staff are short. T45 should set a size per prop (for example ring 10 cm, pills 12 cm, staff and sword 60 cm) and recompute the scale from `bounds_cm`.
- **Triangles:** 7,700 to 8,300 for the Meshy 6 Lite props, 13,400 to 15,650 for the Meshy 7.1 models. The horned helmet (15,654) is the only one a little over the 15,600 bar.
- **Undersides (for T47):** top hat and horned helmet are filled (closed bottoms); bob wig and fedora are open. The others are recorded as `unchecked`: two tries to see them upside down from above missed the frame. The horned helmet's beard and face sit under the helmet, so it would cover a robot's face, as T40 said.

### `M_Clay` (for T46 and T47)

- Built as the sheet said, with the tangent-space normal. It compiles with no `LogMaterial` errors. From the house camera and the close-ups the clay reads as matte; the thumbprint bumps don't show at `ThumbScale` 0.1 and `ThumbDepth` 0.15 under the stage lights. Untuned: T46 should try `ThumbDepth` 0.4 to 0.6 in a close-up on its engine shapes, and `ThumbScale` about 0.5 for shapes at scale 0.5 to 1.5. `TwoSided` was not needed: the inside of the hood, bob wig and dino hood looked fine.
- `MI_Clay_Neutral` is `Color` (0.35, 0.33, 0.31). Under the stage spotlights the headpieces read almost white; T47's role colors will decide the real look.
