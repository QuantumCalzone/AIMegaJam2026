## Findings

### What my person decided

- **The sources.** At first my person chose to generate the music themselves, and Claude wrote a brief for each slot: `Local/audio/candidates/00_INDEX.txt`, `00_TEMPLATE.txt` (a fixed part 1 for loop, tempo, bars and length, plus a part 2 for the style), and one `PROMPT.txt` per slot folder. Then they asked Claude to generate through a service. Claude recommended Stable Audio over ElevenLabs Music and Suno. Stable Audio has a commercial license on every plan, models its site says were "trained on fully licensed material", a set length and BPM, and no rule against games. ElevenLabs' Music Terms of 2026-10-09 exclude "Studio Games" past an indie revenue cap, and WAV downloads start at its $99 plan. Suno's free tier is non-commercial, and its paid tiers cap downloads. My person signed up at platform.stability.ai, OKed $0.08 a take, and put the key in `Local/stability_key.json`. Claude never read the key.
- **"More background."** My person's answer: "a different song for each phase, and for the acting phases, … unique per backdrop so that it kind of matches the vibe of the story". There's no ambience bed.
- **The slots grew from 13 to 16.** The show has three phases that T71 gave no track of their own: Scene Prep (today it plays the card's track), Last Word (today it keeps the Free Talk track) and Reactions (today nothing plays). Each now has its own track. The card's track now starts at The Scene, not at Scene Prep. The fallback for a card with no track (`show`) was optional, and my person picked one.
- **The style.** Round 1 had a different style per slot. Then my person asked for the title to be "more like a grand symphony that would be in a play? Maybe even almost Mozart", and for the scenes to be symphonic film score, with three exceptions: "pop music from a Tarantino movie" for `glowing_briefcase`, "a cyberpunk drum and bass type thing" for `pick_a_pill`, and "almost like folk music" for `league_night`. Later: "redo everything that isn't a scene to symphonic / orchestral", and for the win screen, "Make it more triumphant."
- **The prompts name no composer, film or artist.** My person named film composers as references. Those are the composers of the very films the cards parody, and naming them invites the imitation this task rules out, so the prompts describe the genre and the instruments instead. Mozart is the only name, because his music is centuries out of copyright and isn't a film score.

### How the takes were made and checked

- **Stable Audio 3 through the API** (`POST https://api.stability.ai/v2beta/audio/stable-audio/text-to-audio`, model `stable-audio-3`):
  - It costs a flat 8 credits ($0.08) per take, and failed takes are free.
  - It's asynchronous: the POST returns an id with HTTP 202, and `GET /v2beta/audio/results/<id>` gives 202 until the audio is ready, about 8 s later.
  - A take can be 1 to 380 s long. With `output_format=wav`, it comes back as 16-bit, 44.1 kHz stereo.
  - The API has no loop switch, no negative prompt and no stems. The web app's multi-track mode isn't in the API.
  - The account went to −103 credits before the API refused with HTTP 402, so a new account's 25 free credits can be overdrawn.
- **Every take hit its prompt's BPM exactly**, by an autocorrelation tempo estimate. One take, the round 1 `last_word` take C, drifted to about 115 BPM from 84.
- **It masters loud.** Most takes measured about −14 LUFS, with peaks at full scale and often clipped samples. Round 1 had up to 51,000 clipped samples in one take. A prompt with a "heartbeat kick drum" gave 51,000 to 80,000 clipped samples in all three of its takes. The orchestral prompts clipped far less, mostly under 1,000 samples. The picks' clipped-sample counts are in the shortlist.
- **Prompts describe what to include, never what to leave out.** A text-to-audio model can read "no vocals" as "vocals", so each prompt says "instrumental", "steady and even", "soft dynamics" and "underscore". None of the 16 picks was flagged for vocals, by my person's ear.
- **Loops.** Each take was generated at 120 s, longer than its loop, and the loop was cut from its steady middle:
  - The search compares 32-band log spectra in 6 s windows around each candidate start and end point, with lengths within ±15% of the slot's bar target. The best match always fell on whole bars.
  - The end is moved to the sample with the best waveform match.
  - The last 0.25 s is blended linearly with the audio just before the start, so the end runs into the start. An equal-power blend clipped two loops, so it was dropped.
- **Measurements on the pick page.** These are clipped samples in the take, the spread of 3 s short-term loudness (5th to 95th percentile), and how the seam's surroundings match. The page flagged anything over the limits, and my person listened to each flag before picking.
- **The pick page** was a private Artifact, "NHOS Music Cue Sheet" (https://claude.ai/artifact/KLbXocMcDnvR13B2UyzAdN). It was laid out as a film music cue sheet with the usage codes MT, BI and ET. Each take had a 45 s preview at −20 LUFS, built from the loop's last 15 s and then its first 30, so the seam plays at 0:15. Each also had its full loop, leveled the same way, on repeat.
  - Artifacts don't serve `.m4a`. AAC audio renamed to `.mp4` works.
  - The `assets` capability takes no audio, so the previews went up as files published with the page.
- **The scripts** (`stable_audio.py`, `loopcut.py`, `build_page.py`, `finish.py`, `prompts.json`) ran from this session's scratchpad. This Mac has no ffmpeg, so they used `afconvert`, `afinfo` and numpy in a scratch virtualenv. A hook blocks Claude's Write tool in the repo folder from a worktree, so only the takes and briefs live in the repo folder's `Local/`. A copy of the scripts is in the shared notes folder of the session that started this one.
