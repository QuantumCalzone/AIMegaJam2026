## Findings

### What my person decided

- **The sources:** "Both": free licensed libraries for the short foley and the clicks, and Stable Audio for the stings and the crowd. The Stable Audio account is T100's, with the same terms (we own the output, a commercial license, no credit needed). The account ran out of credits after 19 takes (HTTP 402, "You lack sufficient credits"). My person topped it up, and `GET https://api.stability.ai/v1/user/balance` showed 105 credits before the last 3 takes.
- **Three cues took more than one round.** Each time all three takes were turned down, I asked what to change, in a question with options, and my person wrote their own answer:
  - **The human sting** (you are cut, so you lose): "It should be on the sad side since it means the player lost." Round 2 was three sad orchestral takes. My person picked E.
  - **The AI sting** (an AI is cut, so you survive): "It should be a mix of triumphant for you and also a little silly for the bots being mistaken, which is funny." My person picked F from round 2.
  - **The hover:** round 2 was softer switches from the click's own pack ("Softer sibling of the click", my recommendation), and my person turned those down too: "This sound should be a really soft hint. All it's supposed to do is indicate that your mouse is over an interactable element. Nothing mechanical, really, sounding to it. Maybe something airy." Round 3 was three airy sounds made by script, presented 10 dB softer than the rest of the page. My person picked I, a tiny rising whoosh of air.
- **The drum roll loops.** My person picked B, a 17 s take. In the logs of recent shows the vote runs 25 to 50 s from `NHOS: SOUND drum` to `NHOS: SOUND sting_*` (T20's 18.8 s roll ran out too). B repeats every 4 s (envelope autocorrelation 0.72 at 4.000 s), so I cut three of its bars (0.97 to 12.97 s) into a 12 s loop, blending the 50 ms after the end into the start; the seam's spectra match at r = 0.992. My person heard it played three times and chose "Use the loop".
- **The voices:** my person picked against my recommendation for Anthropic (A, T72's set, not B) and Meta (B, not C). They then heard the four picked sets and their own voice one line after another and said each is easy to tell apart.
- My person picked against my recommendation seven times out of 16 cues: the drum roll, both stings' round 2, the click, the hover's round 3, and the Anthropic and Meta voices. My recommendations came from the sources' descriptions, the prompts and the measurements, because I can't hear.

### How the candidates were made

- **Free libraries.** kenney.nl answers automated requests with HTTP 403, so the Kenney packs came from Kenney's own uploads on OpenGameArt (`RPG Audio`, `Interface Sounds`, `UI Audio`, `Casino Audio`). The other OpenGameArt packs are CC0 by rubberduck, artisticdude, StarNinjas, tinyworlds and others. Wikimedia Commons files came through its API with their license fields. upload.wikimedia.org refused Python's `urllib` with HTTP 429 even after minutes of backoff, but served the same files to `curl` at once. Only CC0, CC BY and public-domain files were used. CC BY-SA files were left out.
- **Stable Audio 3** through the API, as T100 found it: a POST to `/v2beta/audio/stable-audio/text-to-audio` returns 202 and an id, and the result comes from `/v2beta/audio/results/<id>` in about 8 s. Takes of 2 to 25 s, at a flat 8 credits each. Short foley came out as several hits in one take (the 8 s steps take had about 17), so single hits were cut from the loudest isolated peaks. Like T100's music, every take came out at about −14 LUFS with peaks at full scale.
- **The voices** are made by `Tools/make_voices.py --variant b` and `--variant c`, new makers beside T72's (variant `a`), each with its own seed offset, so every run writes the same bytes. Variant `a` still writes T72's files byte for byte (checked against the committed files). The plain run (`--variant picked`, the default) writes the picked set of each robot. Two runs gave the same hash, `49996827…`. The pitch order holds in every variant (Meta lowest, then Anthropic, then OpenAI, then SpaceXAI), with the same base pitches as T72. The numbers for the picked sets, from the script:

  | Set | Clips | Length s | Centroid Hz | Flatness | <300 Hz | 300 Hz-1 kHz | 1-3 kHz | 3-8 kHz |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | OpenAI B | 10 | 0.088-0.121 | 1138 | 0.002 | 16.0% | 49.7% | 26.3% | 7.8% |
  | Anthropic A | 10 | 0.091-0.127 | 485 | 0.000 | 8.6% | 87.3% | 4.0% | 0.0% |
  | SpaceXAI B | 11 | 0.075-0.108 | 1104 | 0.013 | 7.4% | 63.8% | 21.5% | 6.4% |
  | Meta B | 10 | 0.101-0.136 | 558 | 0.000 | 8.9% | 85.3% | 5.8% | 0.0% |

  The script's pitch estimate reads Meta B at 61 Hz with 40% "pitch moves", because the 55 Hz ring modulation puts sidebands around its flat 122 Hz pitch. Every clip is still at T72's weighted loudness, −25.5 dB.
- **Even loudness.** Every candidate was cut, faded and set to the same maximum momentary loudness (BS.1770 K-weighted, 400 ms windows): −20 LUFS, with peaks kept under −1 dBFS. A few very short clicks hit that peak cap first and sit a little quieter. The voice samples all took one fixed gain, so the sets kept their T72 levels against each other.
- **The voice samples.** For each set the page played a sample line in the persona's style, typed out on screen as it played. The audio was made the way T73 plays a line: 30 letters a second, a 0.15 s pause after `, ; :` and 0.3 s after `. ? !`, one sound per vowel run (a final silent e not counted), at most 12 a second, a random pitch of ±6% (played faster or slower, as Unreal's pitch multiplier does), never the same clip twice in a row, and each new sound stopping the last. The text followed the audio from the same letter times.
- **The page** was a private Artifact with each cue's three takes side by side, grouped as the task groups them, each take's source, license, credit line and prompt under it, a "Walk" button for the step sets, and the page republished with each pick marked. Long takes went up as AAC in `.mp4` files and short ones as `.wav`. The publish refused a `contentType` of `audio/mp4` on an `.mp4` file ("already served as video/mp4"), and took both kinds with no `contentType`.
- **The scripts** (`sa.py`, `au.py`, `candidates.py`, `sample.py`, `cast.py`, `build_page.py`, `gen_hover.py`) ran from this session's scratchpad in a virtualenv with numpy and scipy, with `afconvert` for decoding, since this Mac has no ffmpeg. A copy is in the shared notes folder of the session that started this one. The hover's script, `gen_hover.py`, is also in `Local/audio/candidates/t101/`, with the takes and notes.

### For T102

- **Folders and names:** `SourceAssets/Audio/show/`, `robot/` and `ui/` hold one WAV per asset, named for it (`S_DrumRoll.wav` and so on). The folders are lowercase, unlike the task's `Show/`, `Robot/`, `UI/`, `Voice/`, to match `music/` and `voices/` (Mac and Windows can't hold `Voice/` beside `voices/`). The voice files replaced T72's in place, with the same names and counts, so `LoadVoices` and `Syllable` need no change; only OpenAI, SpaceXAI and Meta's waves need reimporting.
- **`S_DrumRoll` is a loop.** Set the sound wave to loop. `StopDrum` already stops it at the reveal.
- **`S_TalkBlip` goes.** Nothing plays it: T73 removed `LineBlip` and `Blip`, and a byte search of `Content/` finds it only in `BPC_ShowAudio`, under `Sfx`'s "blip" branch, which no caller uses. Delete the asset, that branch (and its lines in `Graphs/BPC_ShowAudio/Sfx.txt`) and its row in `Docs/Assets.md`.
- **Levels.** The files sit at an even −20 LUFS maximum momentary loudness (the hover at −30), not at the game's levels. The task's rule is that nothing is louder than the voices except the stings and the applause at their moments, so set each wave's volume as T20 and T71 did. The hover should sit well under the click: my person asked for "a really soft hint".
- **Stereo.** The show, robot and click files are stereo, and the hover and the voices are mono. All are played in 2D today.
- **Credits:** the applause and the laughs are CC BY 3.0 and need their credit lines in the game's credits and `Docs/ItchPage.md` (in the shortlist). Move the T101 section into `Docs/Assets.md` on import, as T20 and T100's sections say.
