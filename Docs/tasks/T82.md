## Findings

### Decisions (mine, in my person's place)

1. **The material: the textured `M_CastModel`, as the prep sheet recommends.** The textures carry what makes each bot read as its concept: OpenAI's cable-knit turtleneck and cream belly, Anthropic's belly and buttons, SpaceXAI's belly, springs and boots. On `M_Clay` each part would be one flat color and those details vanish. The cost: on the cut the whole textured part goes gray, belly included, where T79 kept the cream belly cream. From the house camera that reads as "this one is out", which is the point.
2. **`Visor` stays, moved back into the recess (the prep sheet said to hide it).** The model's recess is shallow and uneven (its floor about 23.5 cm out at the eyes and 27 cm on a ridge down the middle, the rim at 30 to 32, from z 116 to 139), so the LED eyes had no surface to sit on. The dark `Visor`, moved (−5, 0, −4) so its front sits at x 31 just inside the rim, gives the eyes and mouth T79's convex screen. The recess and the visor are both near black, so they read as one screen. `Face` moved by the same (−5, 0, −4), so every face part keeps its place on the visor, and T75's `ScreenX` still has a screen to follow.
3. **The resting brows came down 3 cm** (Anthropic's `BrowWL`/`BrowWR` to (36.5, ±11, 142) on `Face`, in front of the glasses' lenses for `BrowMinX` 36.3; SpaceXAI's `BrowAL`/`BrowAR` to (34.5, ±11, 141)), and `FaceParts`' `BrowMaxZ` for the round head is 143 (z 139 in the Rig, the recess's top), because the screen ends 9 cm lower than T79's visor did. At the old heights the brows were buried in the shell above the recess.
4. **`FaceRest` reads the shell as an ellipsoid of the model** (centre z 124.6, radii 32 × 35 × 33.4), not `RHead`, which is deleted. Measured from the mesh's vertices (`ProceduralMeshLibrary.get_section_from_static_mesh`).
5. **Shoulders:** OpenAI 24 and SpaceXAI 35 as the prep suggested. **Anthropic 30, not the suggested 40,** with each arm rolled 20° outward on its own component (`AArmXL` roll +20, `AArmXR` −20, composed with the manifest's transform about the pivot): the egg is 27 cm half-wide at the shoulder (z 76) and 37 cm at z 46, so at 40 the shoulder floated 10 cm off the body, and hanging straight at 30 the hand sank into the belly.
6. **SpaceXAI's arm is flipped along its length** (Y scale negated: `SArmXL` (−0.2599, −0.2599, 0.2599), `SArmXR` (0.2599, −0.2599, 0.2599)): with the manifest's transform it hung upside down, the thumbs-up hand at the shoulder and the spring at the bottom. The thumb still points forward.
7. **No cook line.** Every mesh and material is referenced by a `BP_Robot` component, so it cooks through the reference; nothing loads them by path.
8. **OpenAI's arms keep the prep's size** (not slimmed to 0.8): the chunky knit sleeves match openai.png.

### Unreal MCP and editor rules found in this task

- **`execute_tool_script` can drive Simulate in rounds:** in one call, film the round set up by the last call, then write the next round's command file and type `py "<sim.py>"` into the console box (`tb1`). The MCP round trip between setup and capture avoids T80's "capture right after an attach can miss it". Slow the play world (`slomo 0.05`) so emotions hold.
- **`line_trace_component` on an imported Hyper3D mesh hits its simple collision,** not its surface. Read the vertices with `unreal.ProceduralMeshLibrary.get_section_from_static_mesh(mesh, 0, 0)` instead.
- The editor viewport's size changed during the session (3440 × 1638 to 2864 × 978), so `CaptureViewport` framing differs between shots taken at different times; crop to squares for side-by-sides.
- zsh doesn't split an unquoted variable into words; write `${=L}` to pass a list of files.
