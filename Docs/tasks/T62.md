## The input spec (my words, 2026-11-15)

T62 to T66 build this together, and each of them points here. Once step 1 of this task puts it in the design doc's Rules tab, the doc is the source of truth; this copy only records what the five tasks were asked to build.

**Turn input: click-to-build actions plus a speech field**

### The idea
On each scene turn, the player does two things at once. They click to build one action, and they type one optional line of speech. Clicking handles everything physical: actions, props, places and targets. Typing handles only what the character says. One "Action!" button sends both together.

### Layout (bottom of the screen, top to bottom)
1. Preview line, in terminal style. It updates live as the player builds:
   > throw rose at @Meta: "Oops, my servo slipped."
2. Action builder: a row of action buttons, then target chips for the selected action.
   [Move] [Look] [Grab] [Throw] [Give] [Look around] [No action]
3. Speech field: one line with a live word counter, an "Action!" button and the turn timer.
   Say something (optional)...                      12/15   [Action!]   0:21

### Building an action
- Pick one action button. Only its valid targets appear as chips. Everything else is hidden or greyed out.
  - Move: a place (Center, Left, Right, Front, Back, Riser, Prop Table)
  - Look: an actor, a prop or a place
  - Grab: a prop that nobody is holding
  - Throw: a prop you're holding, then a target (an actor or a place)
  - Give: a prop you're holding, then an actor
  - Look around / No action: no targets
- The builder offers only moves the game would accept from an AI. It uses the same rules that check the AIs' tool calls. If you're holding nothing, Throw and Give are greyed out with the tip "Grab something first."
- Props come from the current scene card.
- Picking a different action clears its targets. Clicking a selected chip again deselects it.
- The default is "No action", so a speech-only turn is always valid.
- One action per turn.

### Saying a line
- Keyboard focus stays in the speech field the whole turn. Clicking the builder never steals focus, so the player can type while clicking.
- The line is optional, with a hard cap of 15 words. The counter turns red at 15 and blocks further words.
- Typing @ opens a list of actors by the names on the player's screen (such as @Meta). Each mention is inserted as a chip and sent to the AIs as the actor's Player name (@Player 2).
- The speech field never controls actions. Typing "I throw the rose" only says those words aloud.

### Submitting
- Press "Action!" or Enter. A turn with "No action" and an empty line is allowed. The player simply stands there.
- When the 30-second timer runs out, the turn sends automatically:
  - A finished action is sent as built.
  - An unfinished action (an action picked but its targets incomplete) becomes "looks around", the same fallback a bot gets when its command fails.
  - Whatever is typed in the speech field is sent as is.

### What happens after sending
- The action goes straight into the shared log as a structured action, in exactly the format the AIs' tool calls use. No LLM is involved.
- The speech goes through the input LLM for spelling fixes only, with no changes to wording or tone. If that call fails or is slow, the line is sent as typed so the turn never stalls.
- The log shows every actor's turn the same way: a short narration of the action, then the quoted line. Example: "Player 3 throws the rose at Player 2. 'Oops, my servo slipped.'" On the player's screen, Player names appear as the names they know.

### Other phases
- Free Talk: the builder is hidden. The player gets the speech field plus a Pass button.
- Last word and reactions after the Cut: speech field only, with their own timers.

### Nice to have later
- Clicking props, places or actors on the stage to fill the selected target.
- Number keys 1–7 to pick actions.

### My answers (2026-11-15)

## Task

- **Why:** the input spec above. Today the game accepts looser actions from an AI than the builder will offer me: 1 or 2 actions a turn, a grab of a prop someone else holds, a throw or give of a prop the robot doesn't hold (it grabs it first), a give to a place (a put-down) and a move to an actor; and it can't look at a prop. If my turns followed other rules, how an actor acts would point at the human.
- **Steps:**
  1. **The design doc first.** Show me the edits, and make them after my OK with the Claude Docs connector (see "The design doc follows the game" in the plan):
     - The Rules tab: how the player takes a turn (the whole input spec above, in the doc's own words and form) and the one rule set in steps 2 to 4.
     - The Bot Prompts tab: the `game` lines on actions and the asks' "Fill:" lines from step 4. A prompt changes there first, then word for word in `prompts.json`.
  2. **The rules, in one place.** In `BP_StageDirector`, add three functions. Names are Player names and place and prop names, as a reply writes them.
     - `ItemsFor(Seat, Verb)`: the props a verb may use. grab: the current card's props (the state's `set.props`) that nobody holds. throw and give: the props the seat holds. Any other verb: none.
     - `TargetsFor(Seat, Verb)`: move: the places. look: the actors still on stage other than the seat, the card's props and the places. throw: the actors still on stage other than the seat, and the places. give: the actors still on stage other than the seat. Any other verb: none.
     - `CanDo(Seat, Verb, Item, Target) → Ok, Why`: true when the item is in `ItemsFor` (or the verb takes no item) and the target is in `TargetsFor` (or the verb takes no target). A look with no target is a look around and is always allowed. Any verb other than move, look, grab, throw or give is false.
     T64's builder calls `ItemsFor` and `TargetsFor` for its chips, and T63 calls `CanDo` on my built action, so none of the three may depend on how a reply's text is parsed.
  3. **Every action goes through the check.** `PlayPart` or `DoVerb` calls `CanDo` before it plays an action. A false check makes the robot look around and prints its reason, as an unknown name does today (`Fallback`, `NHOS: ACT <id> look around - (<why>: <part>)`). New: a look at a prop turns the robot toward the prop, wherever it is. Gone: the snatch of a held prop, the grab-first of a throw or give, the put-down of a give to a place, and the move to an actor (`BotCands`).
     - **Who holds what is game state, not animation.** A robot's queue can play an action seconds after the game accepts it, and lines play one at a time (T30). Keep each prop's holder as the accepted actions leave it, change it when the check accepts an action, and read only that in the check and in step 5. Look at how `BP_Prop`'s holder and slot change today (`FlyToComp`'s `NewHolder`, `IsHeld`, `SlotFor`) before you choose where it lives. A cut actor drops what it holds (`DropAll`), and a dropped prop is free. Props go back to the table at each Scene Prep (`ResetStage`), so nobody holds anything when a scene starts.
     - **Two actors may pick the same free prop in one turn.** The first to play gets it, and the second one's grab falls back to a look around. The same holds for me.
     - `PlayPart` reads the `does_templates` wording, so check that every new case still parses. A look at a prop comes out of "looks at {target}" as "looks at rose", and `DoVerb` must find the prop there as well as a place or an actor. If a template must change, change `prompts.json` `does_templates` and `PlayPart` together (T19's note).
  4. **One action a turn, for everyone.**
     - `prompts.json`: the `game` lines on actions say 0 or 1 action, list the verbs with the new rules, and say that a throw or give needs a prop you hold and a grab needs a prop nobody holds. `asks.scene_turn` asks for "actions (0 or 1)". `asks.reaction` and `asks.reaction_cut` ask for speech only, because my reactions after the Cut have no builder (the spec's "Other phases"). So does `asks.last_word`, if T58 gave it an action.
     - Where a reply's actions become `does` (`HandleReply` or the step after it), keep only the first action of a scene turn and none in a reaction or a last word. Print `NHOS: EXTRA <id> <n> dropped` when you drop any. The shared log never holds a dropped action.
  5. **The AIs see who holds what.** `ActorState` adds `holding`, a list of prop names (maybe empty), to each `cast` entry and to `you`, from step 3's holders. Add a `game` line that explains it, and update the actor state's fields in the `prompts.json` `_readme`. T50 found two unused copies of `ActorState`'s node chain under the live one, so trace from the function entry.
  6. **Free mode.** `free_mode.json`: a free actor takes one action at most in a scene turn, and none in a reaction. Its random props make some throws and gives fall back to a look around, which is fine for a free test. The relay bundles this file, so the push that deploys it needs my OK, typed in this session.
  7. **The checker.** `Tools/check_prompts.py`: the sample show's state gets `holding`, and a check that expects 1 or 2 actions now expects 0 or 1 (none in a reaction). Run `--offline` and `--relay --free`.
