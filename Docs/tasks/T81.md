# T81 The cast in Hyper3D, part 1: generate the models from the concept art

Back to [the plan](../Plan.md). This task's title line, **Model and effort**, **Depends on**, **Needs**, **Touches** and any **Waiting:** or **Blocked:** line are in its entry there, under "Open".

## Task

- **Why:** on 2026-11-15 I chose to replace the cast's bodies with models generated in Hyper3D (Rodin), from the same concept images in [Docs/BotArt/](../BotArt/) that T79 and T80 built from. T79 and T80 built all five bots from engine shapes (no Meshy model was ever made for the cast), so they read as parts put together rather than as the clay figures in the concept art. This task makes the models, with no editor. T82 and T83 put them into `BP_Robot`. The `hyper3d` MCP server was set up on the Mac on 2026-11-15 at user scope and signed in with my account; its 12 tools are `rodin_*` (generate, generate_agentic, generate_bang, create_uploads, wait, get_status, get_result and others).
- **Steps:**
  1. **License and cost first.** Read Hyper3D's terms for my account's plan: can we ship the generated models in the game and list them as ours in the submission? If not, stop and tell me. Then tell me the plan before any paid call: the part list (step 2), the tool for each part, an estimate of the credits and a cap. Wait for my OK. That OK also covers downloading each result's files from `rodin_get_result`.
  2. **Parts, not statues.** `BP_Robot` turns the arms on `ArmPivotL` and `ArmPivotR`, bobs the body, slumps it on a cut, and T75 poses the face parts. So each bot is a few static meshes that take the place of today's engine shapes, not one figure. Read T79's and T80's findings and build sheets for the parts and their sizes, and adjust this list where the build differs (say why under "## Findings"):
     - **The shared round head shell,** made once for OpenAI, Anthropic and SpaceXAI: the oval head (70 wide, 66 deep, 62 tall) with the cream ear pods and the gold collar, its screen a plain dark area. The LED eyes and mouth stay engine parts on `Face`, because T75 poses them.
     - **Each body, without its head and arms:** OpenAI's pale-blue teardrop in the gray knit sweater, with its short legs (the glowing floor ring stays an engine part); Anthropic's round orange body with the cream belly, buttons and round feet; SpaceXAI's round lime body with the cream belly, coil-spring legs and chunky boots; Meta's doll torso in the blue tee, hips, legs and cream shoes; my teal cardboard body box with the duct tape and bottle caps, jeans and sneakers.
     - **A left and a right arm for each body,** with the shoulder at one end so it turns on its pivot: OpenAI's sweater sleeves and hands, Anthropic's arms and mittens, SpaceXAI's spring arms, Meta's jointed doll arms, my striped sleeves and garden gloves.
     - **Meta's doll head** (the ears and nose) and **my box head** (the tape on the lid and two open eye slots). Meta's glasses, both bots' eyes and my marker smile stay engine parts: T47's shades hide the glasses and T75 poses the eyes and the smile.
     - Small parts stay engine parts unless one reads much better as a model: SpaceXAI's nose and teeth, Anthropic's glasses, the halo, the warning light and antenna, the bolt, the ping-pong ball, the scarf and Meta's chain.
  3. **Find the method on one bot first: Anthropic.** (a) Generate the whole bot from `anthropic.png` with `rodin_generate`, then split it with `rodin_generate_bang` into head, body and arms. (b) Generate each part from its own reference, cropped from the concept image with one subject per image, as the tools' own descriptions advise. Keep the method that gives clean parts (one closed piece each, a clean cut at the neck and the shoulders) and use it for the other four. Write the comparison under "## Findings".
  4. **Generate the rest.** Upload references with `rodin_create_uploads`. Use `rodin_generate` for the rounded clay parts (the bodies, the round head, Meta's head and arms) and `rodin_generate_agentic` for parts built from flat panels (my box head and box body), as each tool's description advises. Export FBX (GLB if FBX isn't offered) with the model's own texture, about 15,000 triangles a part and no more than 60,000 a bot. Wait with `rodin_wait`. Never retry a timed-out generation on your own (the tools' rule): check `rodin_get_status`, and stay under the cap. No hair, no wires and no brand marks ([BotArt.md](../BotArt.md) rules).
  5. **Save** each part in `Docs/CastArt/<bot>/<part>/` (the shared head in `Docs/CastArt/round_head/`): the model file, its textures, the reference image, a thumbnail and a JSON log of the tool, the settings, the generation id and the credits, as `Docs/PropArt/<id>/` does. Model and image file types already have LFS rules. Add a line to [BotArt.md](../BotArt.md) that points to `Docs/CastArt/`.
  6. **Sheet.** Tile every part's thumbnail beside its concept image (T40 found that a short Swift script with AppKit does this on the Mac with no packages; put the sheet in `Local/art/`, not in git). Check each part: one closed piece, nothing stray, a clean neck or shoulder end, and it reads as its concept image. Redo at most two parts a bot.
  7. **Assets.** Add a row for each part to [Assets.md](../Assets.md) under "Generated 3D models", as the Meshy rows do, with the license step 1 found.
  8. Show me the sheet.
- **Done when:** every part in step 2's list (as adjusted) is in `Docs/CastArt/` with its log and an `Assets.md` row, the credits stayed under the cap, and I say the set looks right.
