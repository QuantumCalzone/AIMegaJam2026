## Art

Everything on stage is built in the editor from the engine's basic shapes (`/Engine/BasicShapes/` Cube, Cylinder, Sphere, Cone) with our own Substrate materials:

- T11: the robots' first look (`M_Robot`, `MI_StageFloor`, `MI_StageWall`).
- T17: the improv theater, the persona robots' parts and the six scene backdrops. `M_Set` (one material for the set and the robots' accessories; its wood planks are drawn by a small HLSL node, not a texture), `M_Backdrop` (a two-color painted sky) and the instances in `Content/NHOS/Stage/Materials/`. No textures.
- T106: the theater's engine parts that stay (walls, floors, the proscenium, the masking, the decks, the box walls, the Riser, the plinths, the pilaster boards, the chandelier's chain, the candle flames and the footlight bulbs), in the six `MI_Theater_*` instances recolored to T98's swatches. The set pieces themselves are T106's generated models (under "Generated 3D models").

The money burn and its provider badges are eight textures in `Content/NHOS/UI/MoneyBurn/`. T99 redrew them on 2026-11-18 from two Leonardo pictures; `Docs/Art/2D/README.md` has how each was made, with the script and the generation ids. (T35 and T36 made the first set; `Docs/MoneyBurnArt.md` keeps their story.)

### Generated 3D models

Made with Leonardo.ai (a reference image, through its paid API, private) and Meshy (image to 3D, on the Pro plan, which gives ownership). `Docs/PropArt.md` has how; each model's folder in `Docs/PropArt/` has its reference image and the JSON logs of its prompts, settings and credits. T43 imported every model as an asset (`Content/NHOS/Stage/Headpieces/`, `Props/<card id>/`, `Set/`, each `SM_<Name>`, with `M_<Name>` and `T_<Name>_BaseColor` for props and set pieces); none is placed in the game yet.

The cast's parts are T103's: made with Hyper3D Rodin on my Business plan, through its MCP server, from T95's approved turnarounds; `Docs/Art/Cast/<character>/<part>/` holds each one's model, textures, crops, thumbnail, turntable and `log.json` (tool, prompt, settings, generation id, dropped tries, credits). They replaced T81's parts in the game on 2026-11-18. T81's files stay in `Docs/CastArt/<bot>/<part>/` as a record, and their rows below are kept with them; none of T81's models is in the game now, and only `T_YouHead_Paint` (T83) still is.

The theater's set pieces and the audience are T106's: made with Hyper3D Rodin on my Business plan, through its MCP server, from T98's approved views (T106's prep, `b8b5feb`); `Docs/Art/Set/<piece>/` holds each one's model, textures, references, thumbnail, turntable and `log.json`. They sit on `M_SetModel` (a Substrate master whose HLSL `PaletteShift` node moves the texture's colors to T98's swatches, no outside code) through one instance each. They replaced T42's set models and T17's, T49's and T86's engine-shape set parts in the game on 2026-11-19; T42's files stay in `Docs/PropArt/` as a record.

The story props (T105) were made the same way, with Hyper3D Rodin on my Business plan from T97's approved views (T97 made them with Leonardo.ai), and replace T41's Meshy props in place: same asset names, new meshes and textures. `Docs/Art/Props/<card>/<prop>/` holds each one's model, thumbnail, turntable and `model_log.json`; `Docs/Art/Props/manifest.json` has each one's bounds, scale and yaw. T41's Meshy files stay in `Docs/PropArt/` as history; no asset uses them now.
